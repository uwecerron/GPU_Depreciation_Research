PROPOSED STUDY PROTOCOL | NOT PREREGISTERED OR EXECUTED
Version: 2026-10-01. Proposed release window: 2026-10-01 to 2027-09-30.
This window is a proposed observation period, not a scheduled autonomous study.

QUESTION AND PRIMARY DIRECTION
Does memory-relieving software improve old-hardware retention margins more
than matched comparison releases under a fixed competitive-price scenario?
Primary mu = weighted paired margin change for memory-relieving releases.
Primary beta = mu minus the corresponding comparison-release mean.
Predict both mu>0 and beta>0. Relative outperformance alone can occur when
both groups lose margin. Positive changes alone need not make retention profitable.
This is a proposed contrast, not automatically a causal estimator: release
selection is observational. Benchmarks can isolate engineering changes;
market outcomes need a separate identification strategy.

BEFORE CONFIRMATORY OUTCOMES ARE INSPECTED
Publish a timestamped inventory specifying all of the following:
- Exact old/new GPU variants, quantities, driver and firmware versions,
  power caps, host/interconnect configuration and initial software commits.
- Workload/model/dataset versions, quality metric and tolerance, context and
  output length, latency percentile target, batch and concurrency settings.
- Release eligibility by documented mechanism and availability/compatibility.
  Classification cannot depend on measured post-release gains. Comparison
  releases must be selected by documented methods, never by observed speedups.
- All workload cells, including failures; fixed sampling weights and strata.
- Baseline memory exposure, baseline economic margin and matching rules.
  Memory-relieving releases and comparisons should share workload targets.
- A single primary utilization/price/cost/salvage scenario with currency and
  time units. Define the mapping from measured new-GPU cost changes to old
  service-price assumptions before seeing margin outcomes. Other pass-through
  assumptions are sensitivities, not alternative primary outcomes.
- Exact statistical estimand, matching algorithm, sample size/power rationale,
  independent release/hardware clusters, interval estimator and alpha level.
  Freeze a simultaneous 95% procedure for the two primary means, for example
  Bonferroni-adjusted 97.5% two-sided intervals for each. Validate the chosen
  cluster-based estimator for the number of independent releases available.
- A held-out workload set and rules for pilot-informed design changes.
A pilot can inform feasibility and power but must be excluded from confirmation.
These fields are not yet populated; the study is not ready to claim registration.

BENCHMARK PROCEDURE
Use matched pre/post commits and fixed targets. Randomize execution order;
record warm-up, thermal state, repetitions and timing distributions. Measure
GPU and stated host energy, throughput and memory. Log every failed run and
why it failed. Kernel incompatibility and quality/latency failure are outcomes,
not reasons to delete a cell. Pair runs on unchanged hardware configurations.
Test competing hardware too. Confirmatory releases must be assessed with the
frozen protocol, including eligible releases that appear unpromising.

OUTCOMES
Primary: mu and beta using best operating-or-idle margin M. For feasible cells:
M=max(0,u*(p*q-c))-F-rho*S; infeasible cells use M=-F-rho*S.
Dollar-valued tier-specific revenues share a common time unit before averaging.
Secondary: positive-margin conversion, break-even service price, throughput,
energy, technical feasibility, and the fraction of preselected workloads
meeting quality and latency. These are alternative uses, not additive capacity.
No extra-years estimate is primary. Any modeled Delta T must show its assumed
price path, future cost path, salvage, uncertainty and model closure.

COMPARISON AND INFERENCE
Do not compare only previously infeasible treatment cells to already-feasible
controls on new-feasibility counts. That would reward the selection rule.
Use baseline memory exposure and profitability to specify strata and match
controls without post-treatment information. Hardware, workload and release
shared effects must be reflected in inference. Repeated timing runs increase
measurement precision; they do not create independent market observations.
With too few independent clusters, use descriptive estimates and uncertainty
ranges, not spurious significance. Report all declared sensitivity scenarios.

INTERPRETATION
Simultaneous lower bounds for both mu and beta above zero: supports the joint
forecast. Either upper bound nonpositive: evidence against it. Other outcomes
are inconclusive. Adequacy and sample-size rules must be met first.
These intervals are conditional on economic scenarios and cannot establish
that real service prices, utilization or switching costs follow those scenarios.
Preservation only at unchanged prices: engineering benefit, not robust
competitive preservation. Power/site scarcity or migration costs that erase
margins weaken the operational interpretation. No adequate data: incomplete.
A positive result does not establish causal market retirement or accounting
useful lives without a matched longitudinal survival study.

LONGITUDINAL EXTENSION
Track device/cohort identities across operators, resale and workload changes.
Define loss of a frontier role before observing exit; do not use model vintage
as a device installation date. Treat unknown destinations as censored rather
than retired. Separate physical failure, voluntary scrapping and resale.
Use actual deployment/adoption timing and appropriate untreated comparisons.
Release dates alone cannot identify a causal retirement effect.

DISCLOSURE
No GPU measurements have been run for this proposed study. The delivered
screening example is synthetic. No study has been publicly registered, no
funding or GPU rental has been committed, and no automated monitoring job exists.
